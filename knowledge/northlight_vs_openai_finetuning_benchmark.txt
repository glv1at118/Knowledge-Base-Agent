This internal note compares Northlight, Vantavo's no-code fine-tuning platform, against the OpenAI fine-tuning API, which is the default choice most prospective customers already know about before they ever hear of Northlight. The goal of this note is to help the sales and product teams understand where Northlight genuinely wins and where it does not, rather than pretending the comparison is one-sided.

Northlight's clearest advantage is that it requires no ML engineering expertise and no data ever leaves the customer's own cloud tenant during training. A legal or professional-services team can prepare a fine-tuning dataset, click through Northlight's guided workflow, and get a working fine-tuned model without writing training code or sending their (often privileged or confidential) documents to a third party's servers. The built-in evaluation suite, which automatically compares the fine-tuned model against the base model on the customer's own sample questions, is also something the raw OpenAI API does not provide out of the box. For compliance-conscious buyers, private-by-default training is frequently the deciding factor, more than raw model quality.

OpenAI's fine-tuning API wins decisively on the underlying model's raw capability. Teams fine-tuning through OpenAI are building on top of far larger, more broadly capable base models than the small and mid-sized open models Northlight currently supports, and for tasks requiring deep general reasoning rather than narrow domain adaptation, that gap in base model quality typically shows up directly in output quality. OpenAI also benefits from a much larger developer ecosystem, extensive public documentation, and a track record of uptime and reliability that a smaller platform like Northlight cannot yet match. Teams that already have in-house ML familiarity often default to the OpenAI API simply because it requires no new vendor relationship.

In an informal internal test, our team fine-tuned both platforms on the same small set of anonymized contract-review questions from a pilot law firm customer. The OpenAI-fine-tuned model scored modestly higher on raw answer accuracy, consistent with its larger base model, but at roughly four times the per-query inference cost once usage scaled past the free evaluation tier. Northlight's fine-tuned model scored slightly lower on the hardest, most ambiguous questions in the test set, but matched OpenAI's accuracy on the more common, routine questions, at a noticeably lower ongoing cost and without any customer data leaving their own environment. This was a small, single-customer, informal comparison, not a rigorous or independently verified benchmark, and the exact numbers should not be quoted externally.

Based on this, the recommended positioning is to lead with data privacy and lower ongoing inference cost for compliance-conscious, document-heavy industries like legal and professional services, rather than trying to win a head-to-head argument about raw model intelligence, where OpenAI's larger models currently have a real and probably durable advantage. Northlight should not be pitched as "smarter than OpenAI" — it should be pitched as "smart enough, private by default, and cheaper to run at scale."
